Little Langdale

Little Langdale, Lake District

My first video, and really a test. This is walk 13 from Lakeland Walking on the Level by Norman Buckley. It is about 3.25 miles and manageable.

The backstory

I'm now 62 and I decided to start a YouTube channel.

I set myself a budget of £1000. I searched YouTube for advice, then chose a phone, a tripod and a gimbal I hadn't used in two years. Then, silly me, I bought a drone.

I bought a walking book from a charity shop. It was all high routes and lots of climbing, which I don't want to do at 62. I then found Lakeland Walking on the Level and chose to start with the easiest walk, walk 13.

The trip

I drove up the night before in my van, Edna, parked near Kendal and slept in the back.

Early the next morning I drove to Little Langdale, arriving at about 7:30am. It was really easy to find, but I drove straight past the parking space without spotting it. When I passed the Three Shires pub I turned round and drove back slowly. This time I saw the gate where the walk starts, and the parking space just after it. Parking is about 300 metres from the pub.

I parked as close to the kerb as I could so other vehicles could get past. I got my equipment ready and folded in my mirror, so I wouldn't come back to a broken mirror on the ground. Then I walked to the start of the walk.

The walk

The walk is about 3.25 miles and manageable, with about 360 feet of rise in total. It isn't flat like a walk around town, but it is doable.

Through the first field you go down a little, then across the field to the bridge and over it. After the bridge there was a bull lying on its belly right next to the path, and a cow on the path itself. The cow wouldn't move, and it took me 10 minutes to get past it. I had a big conversation with that cow, trying to persuade it to move. Eventually it did and I walked on. This isn't in the video, but it was a good experience and it didn't ruin the day.

Once you go through the next gate, the path goes uphill a little. It's manageable, although it was quite overgrown.

When I got to the top of that hill, I met two gentlemen coming down one of the walking paths, which was wide enough for a vehicle. One was walking and one was driving a Transit van. They had got slightly lost and were going diving in the quarry.

I carried on walking uphill, still on an incline, through a gate. When I got to the top I had a decision to make at the road.

You can turn right and come back down. That is the short route and would probably take about 20 minutes, maybe 15. Or you can go almost straight on, up a steeper hill. It is only steep for about 5 metres, and then you walk beside a dry stone wall all the way to the quarry.

I met the two men again when they had found the quarry. I didn't go into the quarry. I'm saving that for next time, because it was drizzling and you have to climb a further hill.

Instead I followed the path that takes you directly to the ford. You don't cross the bridge over the ford to get to Slater's Bridge. Crossing it is an alternative route. I took it, discovered it was the wrong way and had to turn back.

From the ford, follow the path without crossing the bridge. It takes you past some lovely dry stone buildings, typical Lake District buildings made from stone and slate.

Slater's Bridge then comes into view on your right, over the dry stone wall that runs alongside the road. Carry on a little further and you will reach a kissing gate. Go through it and you can walk over Slater's Bridge.

On Slater's Bridge I got the Neo 2 to follow me. It was flying over the water and I was scared it would fall out of the sky and I would lose my drone on day one. It seemed to work okay, though.

In the distance I could see some people walking up another hill. I was worried I would have to walk up it too, as I was getting a little tired by then.

About 10 metres after Slater's Bridge there is a sign that turns right. That takes you back to Little Langdale. It is a pleasant walk and it brings you down some steps right next to the Three Shires pub.

The weather came and went. I had drizzle with a rainbow, then overcast skies with the odd bit of sunshine breaking through, and cloud over the hills.

The book suggests about an hour. It took me 3 to 4 hours because I was filming and learning how to set up my equipment.

I was back at the Three Shires at about 10:30am, but it wasn't open. I rested in the back of the van, then drove home.

It was a really good first adventure. I will definitely be going back, maybe in spring next year, to refilm it when I have more experience with the equipment I'm using.

Kit I used

  • Phone Xiaomi Redmi Note 11
  • Tripod Sirui 5C Traveler, carbon fibre, about 1 kg. Excellent.
  • Gimbal DJI Osmo Mobile 6
  • Drone DJI Neo 2
  • Microphone Hollyland Lark M2

What I learned

The first video was a test and a bit of an adventure, and I really enjoyed the walk. Most of what I learned was about the experience of filming.

I need to plan ahead better. I should check the weather and make sure I have a good map with me. I should plan a script, because I waffled a lot. I should plan my shots. Then I need to practise, practise, practise.

The best camera you have is the one you're carrying with you, and my phone was what I had with me. But the Redmi Note 11 didn't record very well. It used a variable frame rate, which caused problems later, and the footage was mushy and not high quality.

The drone was excellent, but I should have practised with it before the walk. Before flying, I got my licence, my operator ID and insurance.
